# Scene Brief: What Survives a Collision?

**Format:** Vertical 1080x1920 · **Target length:** 60–70 seconds · **Voiceover:** On

## Hook (first 3 seconds)

A two-kilogram cart rushes toward a stationary one-kilogram cart. The frame
freezes just before impact: “Everything is about to change… except one total.”

## Core idea

In a closed system, each object's momentum may change during a collision, but
the vector sum stays constant:

\[
m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 = m_1v_1 + m_2v_2
\]

Here, \(u\) means before and \(v\) means after. Direction is represented by the
sign of velocity.

## Why this idea, this way

Animation lets the viewer watch velocity arrows transfer and change while a
single total-momentum bar remains fixed. The invariant becomes visible rather
than merely stated.

## Audience & assumed background

High-school beginners who know mass and velocity, but have not yet internalized
momentum or signed direction.

## Narrative arc

- A fast cart meets a stationary cart; predict what survives the impact.
- Build momentum visually as mass times velocity.
- Freeze before impact and add the two signed momenta.
- Let the carts stick together; individual velocities change, but the same
  total reappears after impact.
- Generalize from the numerical example to the symbolic conservation equation.
- End with the distinction that \(m_1v_1=m_2v_2\) can describe a special
  equality of momentum magnitudes, but is not the general conservation law.

## Key visuals

- Two clean carts with mass labels and directional velocity arrows.
- Horizontal momentum bars whose signed lengths add to one persistent total.
- A brief impact ripple followed by the carts moving together.
- The numerical balance transforming into the general before/after equation.

The main example is a perfectly inelastic collision: a \(2\,\mathrm{kg}\) cart
at \(3\,\mathrm{m/s}\) hits a stationary \(1\,\mathrm{kg}\) cart, then the
combined \(3\,\mathrm{kg}\) mass moves at \(2\,\mathrm{m/s}\). Both sides total
\(6\,\mathrm{kg\,m/s}\).
