# What Survives a Collision?

In this collision, everything changes—except one invisible total.

Momentum is mass times velocity. More mass or more speed means more momentum,
and direction gives it a sign.

Before impact, the two-kilogram cart moves right at three meters per second. The
one-kilogram cart is still.

Together, their momentum is six kilogram-meters per second, pointing to the
right.

They collide, stick together, and continue as three kilograms moving at two
meters per second.

Multiply again: three times two is six. The motion changed, but the total
momentum did not.

That is conservation of linear momentum. With no significant outside push,
total momentum before equals total momentum after.

So m one v one equals m two v two can describe a special balance. The general
law preserves the total, including every velocity's direction.
